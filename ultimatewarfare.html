THE ULTIMATE TACTICAL WARFARE GUIDE

Welcome to the comprehensive master handbook for army formation, stat interactions, hero synergies, and robot power armor mechanics. This guide explains the core systems in straightforward language and uses practical combat examples.

1. The Troop Triangle & Targeting Mechanics

Combat revolves around a strict Rock-Paper-Scissors hierarchy among three distinct troop types. Banners and positions are locked automatically by the system; your control lies entirely in troop composition ratios.

Shield Bearers Tanks
COUNTERS

Shield Bearers

  • Role : Primary damage absorbers (Tanks).
  • Targeting Priority : Shield Bearers → Bombers → Shooters.
  • Strengths & Weaknesses : Excellent against Bombers; weak against Shooters.

Bombers

  • Role: Balanced units specialized in backline disruption and tactical bursts.
  • Targeting Priority : Shield Bearers (or 20% chance to jump directly to Shooters) → Bombers → Shooters.
  • Strengths & Weaknesses : Excellent against Shooters; weak against Shield Bearers.

Example 1: If your enemy relies heavily on Bombers to deal damage, sending a heavy front line of Shield Bearers will mitigate incoming hits and absorb the assault.

Example 2: If you send Shield Bearers against an army composed mostly of Shooters, your Shield Bearers will melt rapidly because Shooters naturally shred their defense.

Shooters

  • Role : Main DPS (Damage Per Second) carry.
  • Targeting Priority : Shield Bearers → Bombers → Shooters.
  • Strengths & Weaknesses : Great at melting Shield Bearers; weak against Bombers.

    • Example 1: Facing a heavy turtle setup with max Shield Bearers? Loading your march with Shooters will shred their massive health pool faster than any other unit.

    Example 2: If enemy Bombers bypass or break through your frontline, your Shooters will take massive counter-damage due to their structural weakness against Bombers.

    2. Stat Interactions & Damage Dynamics

    Understanding how stats balance each other is essential for optimizing build power thresholds.

    Stat What It Does
    Attack (ATK) Dictates raw offensive damage. Higher ATK forces more enemy troops to trigger a "kill check." Defense (DEF) Directly mitigates incoming raw damage from enemy ATK. Health (HP) Dictates individual unit hitpoints. High HP keeps troops fighting longer and directly prevents troop death. Lethality A specialized damage parameter that bypasses standard defense armor to lower the threshold needed to permanently incapacitate/kill a unit. Tactical Damage Flat/extra damage triggered when specific skill conditions are met. Calculated completely independently from standard basic attack calculations and added on top of total damage.

    How Stats Counter Each Other

    • HP + DEF vs. Attack: High HP and DEF scale together to reduce incoming raw damage and extend your march's battle lifetime.
    • HP + DEF vs. Lethality: High HP and DEF raise the Lethal Damage Threshold, making it significantly harder for enemy Lethality rolls to succeed.

    Example 1 (Stat Countering): If an opponent stacks massive Lethality to force instant kills, increasing your army's HP and DEF raises the execution threshold. This prevents the opponent's Lethality roll from initiating troop loss checks.

    Example 2 (Tactical Damage): A hero ability dealing "150% Extra Tactical Damage" calculates that damage separately from basic armor calculations. Even if the enemy has massive basic DEF, the Tactical Damage lands clean flat damage on top of your normal attack turn.

    3. Deciphering the Battle Report

    After every clash, battle details break down troop states into five distinct categories:

    Report Term Definition Impact on Player
    Total Troops Entire force brought into battle. Starting baseline. Loss Troops permanently killed in action. Cannot be recovered; requires new troop training.
    Heavily WoundedTroops whose HP reached 0 and were sent to Hospital.Recoverable via resources and time (or Recruitment Hall if Hospital is full).
    Lightly WoundedTroops that took partial damage but avoided Hospital.Recover 100% HP automatically upon returning to base.
    SurvivedTroops that took zero damage during combat.Ready for immediate deployment.

    Example: Out of a 100,000 troop march, a battle report showing 10,000 Lightly Wounded, 20,000 Heavily Wounded, and 0 Loss means you suffered zero permanent casualties. Your 10,000 Lightly Wounded are instantly healed at base, while your 20,000 Heavily Wounded reside safely in your Hospital.

    4. PvP Battles & Defender's Advantage

    PvP encounters follow a strict turn-based framework with specific positional rules and defensive safety nets.

    Turn Mechanics & Position Rules

    1. Initiator Advantage: The attacking player always moves first in Turn 1.
    2. Hero Execution Order: Heroes act based on their order in your formation, moving from Left to Right (Leftmost Hero moves first).
    3. Passive Warskill Activation: Any passive warskill activates immediately at the start of that hero's action step before launching an attack.
    4. 40% Incapacitation Cap: Battles end instantly when 40% of either side's total troops are incapacitated.

    Attacking vs. Defending Dynamics

    Attacker Lethality→Checks Defender HP/DEF→Hospital Space Available?
    YES: Converted to HEAVILY WOUNDED (0 Loss in Report) NO: PERMANENT LOSS
    • Defender's Advantage: When defending your base, any troop that would normally die due to enemy Lethality is automatically converted into a Heavily Wounded troop—as long as your Hospital has available capacity.
    • The "Hidden Kill" Effect: As a defender, your post-battle report will display Zero Loss even if the attacker stacked high Lethality. However, those kills are recorded in the attacker's detail metrics as "should be killed troops" that were redirected to your Hospital.
    • Full Hospital Penalty: The moment your Hospital reaches 100% capacity, Defender's Advantage deactivates. All subsequent lethal hits instantly convert to permanent Loss.
    • Defender's Lethality: A defender's Lethality operates at 100% full fatality against an attacker from turn 1, inflicting instant permanent kills relative to the attacker's HP and DEF.

    Example 1 (Defending): You get attacked by a high-Lethality player. Your battle report shows 0 Losses and 15,000 Heavily Wounded. Because your Hospital was empty, the game converted 15,000 lethal kills into hospital admissions.

    Example 2 (Attacking): You launch a solo attack against an enemy base. Because you are the attacker, you do NOT get Defender's Advantage. Any lethal damage dealt by the defender instantly results in permanent Loss on your report.
